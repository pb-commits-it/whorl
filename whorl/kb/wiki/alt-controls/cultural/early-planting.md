# Early planting — cultural

Adjusting planting date to **advance the crop's reproductive window
ahead of peak adult moth flight** is one of the most reliable
non-chemical controls for *Helicoverpa zea* in Kansas corn.

## Mechanism

*H. zea* adult flights peak from mid-July through mid-August in most
of Kansas. Corn that has finished silking before peak flight presents
a much smaller window for egg-laying on silks, lowering season-long
larval pressure on ears.

## Practical implementation

- Plant **2 weeks earlier** than the regional median, conditional on
  soil temperature ≥ 50 °F at 2-inch depth and 5-day forecast trend
  warming
- Pair with early-maturity (95–105 day) hybrids in the Northern
  Kansas band; full-season hybrids (110+) in the South
- Re-check the 30-day forecast — early planting into a cool,
  wet stretch can cause emergence problems that outweigh the
  pest-escape benefit

## Limitations

- Doesn't help against ear-stage pests that overlap with later silk
  windows (e.g. fall armyworm in late-planted refuge corn)
- Requires multi-season commitment to be measurable
- Yield trade-offs in specific drought / heat patterns
