# Helicoverpa zea — corn earworm

The most economically important above-ground corn-ear pest in the Midwest.
Adults are tan-to-brown moths active at dusk; eggs are laid singly on silks
and ear-tip leaves; larvae feed in the silk channel and on developing
kernels.

## Identification

Larvae 1–4 cm long, head capsule tan, body color highly variable (green to
brown to nearly black) with light dorsal and lateral stripes and dark
tubercles bearing single setae. Larvae found in the ear tip during silk →
dough stages are almost always *H. zea*, since *Ostrinia nubilalis*
(European corn borer) bores into the stalk instead.

## Economic thresholds

- **Kansas field corn, silking → dough:** treat at **1 larva/ear** or
  **5 % infested ears** sampled across the field; resampling at 5 days
  recommended if marginal `[ksu-mf743, p.12]`.
- Yield loss roughly 1–3 % per larva per ear when feeding is confined to
  the tip; multiply that per-ear loss across the infested fraction of the
  field.

## Recommended controls — rotate MOA between generations

### Chemical

- [[products/spinosad]] — **IRAC 5** — REI 4 h, PHI 1 d. Excellent
  efficacy on L1–L3 larvae; less reliable on L4+. Selective; preserves
  beneficial arthropods.
- [[products/chlorantraniliprole]] — **IRAC 28** — REI 4 h, PHI 1 d.
  Long residual; good ovicidal-larvicidal activity when applied at
  early egg lay.
- [[products/bifenthrin]] — **IRAC 3A** — REI 12 h, PHI 1 d. **Avoid in
  known pyrethroid-resistant counties (Southern Plains).** Use only if
  no IRAC 3A application has been made on this field in the current
  pest generation.

### Biological

- [[alt-controls/biological/bt-kurstaki]] — selective for Lepidoptera,
  most effective on L1–L2 larvae <½ inch. Apply at evening to mitigate
  UV degradation.

### Cultural

- [[alt-controls/cultural/early-planting]] — early planting escapes
  peak adult flight in Kansas by ~2 weeks in most seasons.
- Refuge compliance for Bt-transgenic varieties (when planted) is the
  single most important resistance-management practice; pyramided
  Bt-traits (Cry1A.105 + Cry2Ab2) provide stronger and more durable
  control than single-trait events `[ksu-mf743, p.13]`.

## Resistance status — KS, NE

Pyrethroid (IRAC 3A) tolerance has been documented in *H. zea*
populations across the Southern Plains. KS counties south of I-70
should default to IRAC 5 or 28 unless a recent susceptibility bioassay
indicates otherwise. **Never apply two consecutive same-MOA sprays in a
single generation** [[moa/irac-3a-pyrethroids]] [[moa/irac-5-spinosyns]].
